The method, for analysts and hiring teams

I'd like to work on recruitment models and the visual side of how clubs read them. In October 2026 I rebuilt my whole event-data pipeline after finding bugs in the coordinate frame, the match clock and the VAEP formula, and re-ran everything on top of it. My transfer-corridor piece retracted one of its own March claims. That's how I'd work on your data too.

  • Transfer success by league

    Six seasons of moves against an intra-league control group: which leagues' players hold their level, and why most failures are about minutes.

  • Fair transfer price

    Worth learned from market values, then calibrated on 3,593 real fees: selling league, age, window, football inflation. Backtested window by window.

  • Squad needs

    What a squad is short of, read from its own numbers: a quality that lags what a paired quality implies, checked against teams that play the same way and kept only if it repeats.

    Read October 2026

  • Position clustering

    Roles from where and how players act, not from team sheets: the role behind every comparison I make.

  • Team style similarity

    Which teams play like yours, from possession and pressing maps, so needs are measured against real peers.

  • Dixon-Coles ratings

    Team strength from my own xG model plus the threat a team builds before the shot, comparable across leagues: how I translate a player's numbers from one league to another.

  • Defensive value

    What a defender's zone concedes, from convex hulls and VAEP. Event data struggles with defending, and the method says where.

  • Standstill take-ons

    Two proxies for beating a man from a standing start. The simple one was the more repeatable.

    Read February 2026, before the rebuild

  • Explosive carries

    An acceleration proxy from event data, compared against Gradient's tracking data: the La Liga outliers largely match.

    Read February 2026, before the rebuild

  • Passing skill

    A Bayesian model of how much better than expected a player passes, with shooters and keepers in the same framework.

  • Tracking data

    Peak velocity, and why one number isn't enough; off-ball runs three seconds before the pass. Both on Gradient's free World Cup 2022 data.
